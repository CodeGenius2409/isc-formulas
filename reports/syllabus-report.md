## How entries were classified

- **Core:** in the syllabus, and likely to be used. Everything the solver can calculate, or whose name appears in a paper from 2023 onward, is core.
- **Supporting:** in the syllabus but minor or derived, such as lists of units and constants.
- **Out of syllabus:** not covered by the 2028 document. Each has a reason below.
- **Not needed:** trivial, or not something a student looks up.
- **Uncertain:** kept in the app, and listed in `reports/syllabus-review.md` for you to decide.

**A conflict in my instructions.** You said anything in the papers is core. But the papers go back to 2017, and the 2028 syllabus has since dropped topics they tested, such as radioactivity and solid state. So when the syllabus leaves something out and a paper includes it, I did not call it core and did not remove it. It went to your review list. A name match is also only a signal: it can be a generic phrase.

**A limit on the evidence.** Board papers test Class XII, so a Class XI topic missing from them proves nothing. Those decisions rest on the syllabus text alone.

Mapping was decided at subtopic level for all 518 subtopics, after reading every formula name, with name-based rules on top. `archive/classification.json` holds the outcome and syllabus topic for every id.
